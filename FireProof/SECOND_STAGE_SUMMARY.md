# Second-Stage Scientific Summary

## 1. Strongest unique prediction

The strongest comparatively distinctive prediction is not the `2/3` exponent.
It is the joint state closure

```text
M/A^(2/3) = beta0 [2CF/(C+F)]^2,
C'/C = alpha(1-C)F-mu,
```

together with the resulting prospective sign condition for `M'`. Competing
models can reproduce the slopes much more easily than both state equations.

## 2. Strongest falsification

Independently measure active connectivity and remaining connected fuel, fit
parameters only on development fires, and test held-out fires. Systematic
failure of the recovered `beta0` to remain stable, or repeated wrong signs for
next-period acceleration from the peak balance, would strongly weaken the
closed theory even if the perimeter-area slope remains near `2/3`.

## 3. Redundant headline predictions

`P~A^(2/3)`, `A~t^3`, and `P~t^2` are not three independent results. Their
exponents satisfy `2=(2/3)*3`; any two imply the third under stable
coefficients. Moreover, the time laws apply only to the constant-forcing
special case, not the full life-cycle trajectory.

## 4. Unidentified states and parameters

Area identifies only `beta0CFeta`. If perimeter is truly active perimeter, area
plus perimeter identify `kC` and `(beta0/k)Feta`. Without independent `Amax`,
active boundary, and `k`, the individual `beta0`, `C`, `F`, and `eta` are not
recoverable from `A(t)` and `P(t)` alone.

## 5. Is the life cycle guaranteed?

No. The exact balance permits initial acceleration or initial decline. The
750-run sweep contained 312 monotone-decline trajectories and 412 interior
global peaks. Rise-peak-decline occurs in part of parameter and initial-state
space. Peak uniqueness was not proved.

## 6. New manuscript prediction

The smooth closed model predicts asymptotic, not finite-time, extinction from
positive states. Therefore an abrupt physical ending above the observation
floor is evidence for a missing process such as a barrier, weather transition,
fuel discontinuity, suppression, or a detection model. This prediction should
be stated and tested explicitly.

## 7. Smallest discriminating empirical analysis

Use fires with repeated area, independently measured active perimeter, and a
prefire connected-fuel mask defining `Amax`. Calibrate `k`, `beta0`, `alpha`,
and `mu` on development fires; then predict next-period acceleration sign and
peak crossing on held-out fires. Compare with persistence, a flexible
trajectory model, and a conventional weather/fuel spread baseline. This tests
the state mechanism rather than re-counting dependent scaling slopes.
