# Ranked Empirical Discrimination Tests

The ranking emphasizes mechanism discrimination, not convenience.

## 1. Held-out state-closure and peak-balance test

**Theoretical prediction:**

```text
M/A^(2/3) = beta0 [2CF/(C+F)]^2
C'/C = alpha(1-C)F-mu
sign(M') = sign(B).
```

**Required observations:** subdaily or daily area, independently estimated
active connected fireline, event-specific burnable area `Amax`, and enough
pre-peak observations to estimate derivatives. Fit `beta0`, `alpha`, `mu`, and
any active-line calibration only on development fires.

**Competing explanation:** flexible trajectory models and conventional spread
models can reproduce area growth without the two state closures.

**Support:** event-constant recovered `beta0`, linear connectivity balance, and
held-out prediction of the sign change in `M'` and peak timing.

**Falsify or weaken:** systematic closure residuals, wrong prospective
acceleration sign, or no improvement over simpler baselines after measurement
uncertainty is propagated.

## 2. Prospective forcing-collapse test

**Theoretical prediction:** after dividing out `A^(2/3)`, rates from different
sizes and stages collapse onto `[2CF/(C+F)]^2` up to an event- or fuel-class
constant `beta0`.

**Required observations:** `A`, `A'`, active `C`, and independently delineated
remaining connected fuel `F`.

**Competing explanation:** weather-driven spread, suppression, and fuel-type
variation can generate time-varying effective coefficients.

**Support:** a stable calibrated coefficient with small, unstructured held-out
residuals.

**Falsify or weaken:** residuals retain systematic dependence on wind, fuel,
suppression, stage, or fire size after uncertainty is accounted for.

## 3. Connectivity nullcline and threshold test

**Theoretical prediction:** `C` increases exactly when `(1-C)F>delta`; the
positive quasi-equilibrium disappears when `F<=delta`.

**Required observations:** repeated active-connectivity estimates and fuel
fraction, with sufficient resolution around the predicted crossing.

**Competing explanation:** connectivity is a geometric summary responding to
wind direction, spotting, barriers, or mapping artifacts rather than the
mass-action equation.

**Support:** held-out sign changes in `C'` align with the calibrated nullcline.

**Falsify or weaken:** repeated connectivity recruitment when `F<=delta`, or
decline well inside the predicted recruitment region without measured omitted
forcing.

## 4. Asymptotic-tail versus abrupt-termination test

**Theoretical prediction:** the unchanged smooth model approaches extinction
asymptotically and does not hit `C=0` or `F=0` in finite time.

**Required observations:** high-frequency late-stage fire radiative power,
active detections, perimeter activity, weather, barriers, and suppression
records through termination.

**Competing explanation:** detection thresholds can turn a smooth tail into an
apparently abrupt terminal day; suppression and barriers can create truly
abrupt truncation.

**Support:** resolved smooth decay compatible with the predicted state balance.

**Falsify or weaken:** abrupt physical termination above the sensor floor in
the absence of an exogenous event. Frequent abrupt endings would show that an
important process is missing from the closed model.

## 5. Active-perimeter versus mapped-perimeter test

**Theoretical prediction:** `Pa=k C A^(2/3)` for the active connected boundary,
not necessarily the cumulative mapped perimeter.

**Required observations:** co-registered active-fire detections or incident
fireline mapping and conventional polygon perimeter.

**Competing explanation:** the apparent exponent is generated by inactive edge,
holes, merging, or resolution effects.

**Support:** the active perimeter obeys the closure more closely and improves
next-period growth prediction.

**Falsify or weaken:** cumulative perimeter performs equally well or better and
the inferred `C` has no independent relation to active-line connectivity.

## 6. Headline exponent test

**Theoretical prediction:** in an approximately constant-forcing interval,
`P~A^(2/3)`, cube-root area is linear, and the corresponding asymptotic time
exponents are three and two.

**Required observations:** high-resolution area and active perimeter over a
prespecified interval.

**Competing explanation:** coefficient drift, accelerating characteristic
length, roughening, and branching can reproduce the same slopes.

**Support:** necessary but weak; the slopes should motivate stronger closure
tests.

**Falsify or weaken:** stable, well-measured exponents incompatible with the
closure during an interval where its factors are independently constant.

## Smallest genuinely discriminating analysis

Use a set of fires with daily or subdaily `A(t)`, active perimeter, and a
prefire connected-fuel mask defining `Amax`. On development events only:

1. calibrate `k`, `beta0`, `alpha`, and `mu`;
2. compute past-only `C=Pa/[kA^(2/3)]` and `F`;
3. predict the next observation's acceleration sign and peak crossing from the
   exact balance;
4. compare against persistence, flexible area-trajectory models, and a
   conventional weather/fuel spread baseline on held-out events.

This is smaller than a full incident reconstruction but tests a mechanism that
the exponent triplet does not. It must not define `C`, tune parameters, and
score the model on the same fire without a held-out layer.
