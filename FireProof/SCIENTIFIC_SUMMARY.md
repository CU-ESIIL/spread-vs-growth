# Scientific Summary

## What does the theory actually prove?

- If area and perimeter share a positive characteristic size with exponents `DA` and `Dh`, then eliminating that size gives exponent `Dh/DA`. Under `DA=2` and `Dh=4/3`, the exponent is exactly `2/3`, with an explicit positive coefficient.
- The chosen matching function lies in `(0,1]` for positive ratio and has its unique global maximum at ratio one.
- The active-boundary substitutions imply the displayed two-thirds growth law algebraically.
- Under positive differentiable area and the exact constant-beta growth law, `A^(1-sigma)` is affine in time. For `sigma=2/3`, cube-root area is affine and the explicit area dependence cancels exactly in its derivative.
- The cubic area and quadratic perimeter statements have rigorous late-time ratio limits. They are not exact unshifted early-time proportionalities.
- The finite-fuel fraction has the stated endpoint values, interval bounds, and derivative.
- The connectivity equilibrium and threshold algebra are correct.
- The metabolic and active-perimeter relative-derivative decompositions follow from product and power rules under strict nonzero assumptions.
- The local-slope decomposition is correct, and the `1/2` plus drifting `1/6` coefficient counterexample is exact.

## What does it assume?

The theory assumes positive quantities, compatible measurement objects, a shared size variable, stable coefficients when fixed exponents are interpreted, differentiability, a regular moving boundary, no untracked recruitment in the reduced closure, and specific fuel/connectivity/matching equations. Dynamical invariance additionally requires existence and uniqueness or an equivalent barrier premise.

## What remains empirical?

Whether wildfire has a stable two-thirds perimeter-area regime; whether a mapped perimeter represents active fireline; whether the latent state measures connectivity; whether the matching function is physically optimized; whether chemical power follows recruited area; and whether the reduced state improves held-out prediction all require independent observations.

## Hidden assumptions uncovered

1. The fuel-ceiling no-crossing statement needs continuity plus uniqueness/barrier conditions.
2. The connectivity `[0,1]` invariance claim has the same hidden ODE well-posedness requirement.
3. Relative/logarithmic derivative formulas require strict nonzero states, not merely nonnegative states.
4. The cubic and quadratic time laws are exact in shifted time; `A proportional to t^3` and `P proportional to t^2` are late-time ratio statements.
5. A variable exponent adds a `sigma' log A` term, so the fixed-exponent transformation cannot be reused silently.
6. A general moving-boundary identity needs substantial regularity and transport theory and is therefore explicitly axiomatized here.

## Incorrect or overstated claims

- “`A` cannot pass `Amax` because the derivative is zero at `Amax`” is incomplete as stated. It becomes valid with continuity and forward uniqueness/barrier assumptions.
- “`[0,1]` is invariant because the vector field has the correct boundary signs” is likewise incomplete as stated.
- Any inference from an observed `2/3` slope to a unique fixed geometric exponent or mechanism is false; the formal counterexample demonstrates this directly.

The rest of the core algebra is internally consistent under its named assumptions. That is a statement about the model, not evidence that wildfire obeys it.
